# Historical Arabic and Arabian sky-evidence synthesis

## Scope and project decision

This synthesis compares bounded, previously studied material from Ibn Qutaybah,
Kunitzsch, Hafez/al-Ṣūfī, and King. It records what those inspected sources support and
where they stop. Existing names appear because earlier source study examined them;
they are not a predetermined research list, approved constellation inventory, or
requirement to confirm an early discussion.

`UFUQ-HISTORICAL-SKY-SCOPE` is the project decision that future work is source-led:
identify attested names, groupings, relationships, directional methods, and seasonal
concepts in the sources; keep source text, interpretation, geography, modern
identification, geometry, and lesson approval separate; preserve aliases and
uncertainty; and require evidence plus human review before lesson approval. This
decision controls UFUQ's research process. It is not historical evidence.

No material reviewed here approves Arabic text, translation, modern identification,
membership, line segment, directional relationship, or lesson route.

## Bounded cross-source findings

Each subsection keeps the ten questions independent: occurrence, apparent referent,
tradition, explicit geography, scholarly identification, proposed modern objects,
exact membership, line drawing, directional use, and lesson approval.

### Banāt Naʿsh greater and lesser expressions

- **Occurrence and apparent referent:** Ibn Qutaybah sections 31–32, in the inspected
  local derivative at discovery pp. 68–69, contain greater and lesser expressions and
  seven-star descriptions. The independent text-layer pass did not reliably recover
  every claimed four/three member detail, so exact membership remains `UNVERIFIED`.
  Kunitzsch entries 55–56 and Hafez pp. 125 and 133 separately report the four/three
  structure, subject to their own secondary-source and review limits. The unstable Ibn
  edition prevents learner-facing citation.
- **Tradition and geography:** the text is in Ibn Qutaybah's Arabic compilation. The
  inspected passages do not explicitly state a Najdi geography; geographic basis is
  therefore `UNKNOWN`, not historically rejected.
- **Scholarly interpretation and modern objects:** Kunitzsch entries 55–56 (printed
  p. 48) analyze the transmitted names and crosswalk history. Hafez's Ursa material,
  including pp. 124–125 and 133 and Tables 18, 19, 24, and 26, offers later scholarly
  HR crosswalks. Those crosswalks are proposals requiring independent catalogue-ID
  validation.
- **Membership, line, direction, and lesson:** the inspected texts support a
  four-plus-three descriptive structure, but the repository has no approved exact HIP
  membership, canonical line drawing, directional route, or lesson content.

### al-jady / al-gady / al-gudayy / al-Juday

- **Occurrence and apparent referent:** Ibn Qutaybah section 31 (local discovery p. 68)
  places the expression with the lesser-group material. Sections 52–53, especially
  local discovery p. 82, include it among relative historical travel cues. The
  derivative did not independently verify the earlier terminal/brightness wording.
- **Variants and interpretation:** Kunitzsch entry 107a (printed pp. 62–63) records a
  reading history involving *al-gady* and later diminutive *al-gudayy*. Hafez uses
  *al-Juday* and proposes an HR 424/Polaris crosswalk in Tables 18 and 24.
- **Independent facets:** the reading, transliteration, gloss, source witness,
  referent, proposed modern object, and geographical basis remain separate. The
  inspected sources do not make Polaris identical to terrestrial True North, supply a
  modern Qibla calculation, establish a canonical line, or approve a UFUQ lesson.

### Cassiopeia and “chair” terminology

- **Occurrence and interpretation:** Hafez uses *Dhāt al-Kursīy* as a Cassiopeia
  catalogue heading on thesis/PDF p. 151. Exact source Arabic, historical membership,
  regional use, and a modern instructional pattern remain unresolved.
- **Claim-specific conflict control:** Kunitzsch entries 148a–149 (printed p. 75)
  analyze *kursī al-jawzāʾ* in Orion/Lepus/Eridanus-related material. That specific
  expression cannot be treated as evidence for Hafez's Cassiopeia heading merely
  because both involve “chair” terminology. Kunitzsch's Cassiopeia-related *al-kaff*
  entries 136a–c (printed p. 72) are a distinct claim.
- **Limit:** this is a lexical and source-specific mapping warning, not disproof of all
  historical Cassiopeia or chair-related claims.

## Agreements

- Arabic transmission language alone does not establish ancient Arabian, indigenous
  Arabian, Bedouin, or specifically Najdi origin.
- Anwāʾ/philological material, Greco-Arabic scholarly catalogue material, later
  crosswalks, and modern pedagogy are different evidence layers.
- Exact Arabic, source witness/edition/location, transliteration, translation, period,
  tradition, and geographic basis must be recorded per atomic claim when available.
- A modern catalogue identification needs its own source or argument and uncertainty.
- Historical/cultural records may link stable catalogue IDs but do not copy catalogue
  coordinates.
- Name occurrence, member identification, canonical line drawing, navigational use,
  and lesson approval are independent claims.
- Automated schema and referential checks cannot confer historical or cultural
  approval.

## Required evidence and future tests

- Verified edition/witness and claim-level page/section/verse/folio/table where
  available.
- Page-image verification for OCR-derived Kunitzsch/King details and qualified review
  of Arabic, German, transliteration, and translation.
- Separate modern catalogue-ID crosswalk with source, uncertainty, and conflicts.
- Independent records for membership, line segments, guidance relationships, and
  lesson routes.
- Negative checks for absent pointers, copied coordinates, silent variant collapse,
  conflated claim facets, unsupported regional promotion, and accidental use of
  *kursī al-jawzāʾ* as Cassiopeia evidence.
- Referential integrity and schema validity as technical checks only, never lesson
  approval.

## Phase 1 boundary

Phase 1 uses neutral HIP identifiers and numerical catalogue semantics. Historical
names, cultural memberships, and pattern edges remain separate evidence-backed
overlays and must not enter technical-spike fixtures. This explicit-invocation skill
does not apply to Hipparcos parsing, proper-motion investigation, Astropy comparison,
coordinate transformation, scene-coordinate validation, neutral star rendering, or
generic pattern-line rendering. A later reviewed overlay may reference stable HIP IDs
without altering the numerical catalogue source record.
